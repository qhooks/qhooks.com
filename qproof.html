How QProof works

Every check is a public read: chain nodes, the token’s own code, its pools and public listings. Nothing is signed or traded. Each line in a report links the reads behind it.

  1. Code is fixedReads the upgradeable-proxy storage slots. If one is set, the code behind the token can be swapped later.
  2. No mint, blacklist, pause or fee switchesScans the deployed bytecode for 43 risky function signatures: minting new supply, blocking wallets, pausing transfers, changing buy and sell fees, trading limits and admin roles.
  3. Liquidity depthReads the deepest pool’s reserves (Uniswap V2, V3 and V4, Solana pools and bonding curves) at one block.
  4. Launch liquidity lockedChecks whether the launch liquidity is burned or held by a contract with no way to remove it.
  5. Buy-and-sell testOn EVM chains it buys from the deepest pool and sells straight back inside one simulated call, with nothing sent. That shows the true tax and fees, and whether selling works at all.
  6. Holder concentrationRanks the top wallets and splits supply into pools, locked, staked, burned and everyone else. Known exchange wallets are named.
  7. Creator trailOn Solana it reads the creator’s token account history to show whether they sold.

What the results mean

Passed
The read came back and found nothing to worry about.
Caution
Something worth a look before you buy, like a young pool or thin liquidity.
Failed
A red flag: a risky switch in the code, a sell that fails, or a large hidden tax.
Not checked
A source did not answer or does not cover this chain. A report with gaps is never called clear.

What QProof is not

  • Not an audit. It reads public data at one block; it does not review every line of code.
  • Not financial advice. A clean report does not mean a token is a good buy.
  • It cannot see the future. Owners, pools and holders can change after the check.
